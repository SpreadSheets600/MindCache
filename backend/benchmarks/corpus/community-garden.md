# Community garden handbook

## Membership and access

Members share responsibility for paths, communal tools and the water supply. New members attend an orientation before receiving a gate code. The code changes at the beginning of each growing season and should not be posted publicly. The garden coordinator keeps a waiting list and offers vacant beds in order of application.

Each household may tend one assigned bed. If a member cannot visit for several weeks, they can ask a neighbour to water it or notify the coordinator. The committee checks in before reallocating any neglected plot. Visitors are welcome while accompanied by a member, and children must be supervised near the pond and tool shed.

## Soil and compost

The garden uses compost to improve soil structure and retain moisture. Finished compost is dark, crumbly and earthy smelling. Spread a thin layer over a bed before planting, keeping it away from the stems of young plants. Do not add large quantities of fresh manure immediately before harvesting edible crops.

The communal compost bins accept leaves, untreated grass clippings, vegetable scraps and small amounts of shredded cardboard. Chop bulky stalks before adding them. Do not add cooked food, meat, dairy products or diseased plants: these can attract pests or spread disease. Alternate damp green material with dry brown material and turn the pile when it becomes compacted.

If the compost smells sour, it is probably too wet or lacks air. Mix in dry leaves and turn the heap. If it remains dry and inactive, add water until the material feels like a wrung-out sponge. The compost team labels finished piles so members know which bin is ready to use.

## Watering

Water early in the morning when possible, directing the flow at the soil rather than the leaves. Deep, less frequent watering encourages roots to grow downward. A mulch of straw or shredded leaves can reduce evaporation, but leave a little space around plant stems to discourage rot.

During a prolonged dry spell, the committee may limit hose use. The rain barrels are reserved for watering plants; close their lids after use to prevent mosquitoes. Report a leaking tap or damaged hose to the coordinator rather than leaving it for the next person.

## Tools and safety

Return tools clean and dry to their labelled hooks. Carry forks and rakes with their points down, and wear sturdy shoes when digging. The shed contains a first aid kit and a notice with local emergency contact details. Members should not operate powered equipment without the training required by the committee.

When lifting bags of soil, use a trolley or ask for help. Lock the shed and gate if you are the last person to leave. Report damaged tools so they can be repaired before someone is injured.

## Planting and harvest

Plan crop rotations so the same plant family does not occupy a bed year after year. Rotate leafy vegetables, legumes and fruiting crops to reduce pressure from soil-borne pests. Keep taller plants on the north side of a bed when practical so they do not shade neighbouring plots.

Harvest only from your assigned plot unless a sign identifies produce as communal. Leave flowering herbs for pollinators and avoid spraying on windy days. The committee holds a seed exchange in spring and a shared harvest meal at the end of summer.
